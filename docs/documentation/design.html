Architecture

The whole system is a build-time pipeline and a static page. Nothing executes on a server when someone visits. 

Eurostat API
    │  extract → parse → normalize → transform → load
    ▼
data/processed/*.parquet  +  *.datamap.json      canonical, versioned
    │  sme_pipeline/export.py   (curated, per-chart)
    ▼
docs/data/{series,labels,meta}.json              ~300 KB gzipped
    │  fetch() at page load
    ▼
docs/index.html + app.js + style.css             GitHub Pages

Why not a database

The canonical tables are 3.1 MB and 1.1 MB of Parquet. A database would add a service to run, a schema to migrate and credentials to manage, and buy nothing: the analytical work happens at build time in pandas, and the page needs a fixed, curated slice rather than arbitrary queries.

Why a static page and not Streamlit

Streamlit's advantage is running Python per interaction. Nothing here needs that — filtering a few thousand rows is client-side work. A static page loads instantly from a shared link, survives being screenshotted, is indexable, and has no process that can be asleep when someone clicks. The distribution mechanism is a link on social media; that argues for static more than any technical consideration.

Why the export exists

The Parquet tables carry 291 firm indicators across 18 different unit bases, most of which must never be charted beside each other. Shipping them whole would be both large and dangerous. The export publishes a curated subset — currently 13 charts, 50 indicators, 43,721 rows — with each chart pinned to exactly one unit.

The rules the page enforces

These are not stylistic preferences. Each one prevents a specific, plausible error that would produce a chart that looks right and is wrong.

One unit per chart, and the base is always stated

Every value in the source is a percentage, but of eighteen different populations. PC_ENT is a share of all enterprises; PC_ENT_AI_EC is a share only of those that considered AI. Each chart declares a single unit at build time, and the export refuses to emit a chart without one.

Barriers use the base the question was actually asked of

Read as a share of all enterprises, "we lack expertise" is 5% and the size bands invert — because most firms never considered AI and so answered nothing. Read as a share of firms that did consider it, the same answer is 59%. The second is the real figure; the first is an artefact of the denominator. Because that base is not the enterprise population, those figures are never weighted.

EU27 is displayed, never recomputed

Eurostat's aggregate is weighted by enterprise counts. A plain mean of the member states is a materially different number. The portal shows the published row.

Multi-country groups are weighted the way EU27 is

Selecting several countries combines them weighted by how many enterprises each has. Checked against Eurostat: all 32 reporters, small firms, 2024, ICT recruitment — weighted 6.50% against Eurostat's published 6.23%, where a plain average gives 7.31%. The residual is the six non-EU reporters the EU27 aggregate excludes.

The weighting decision is made once per series, not per point

Enterprise counts exist only for 2021–2024. Deciding year by year would weight a trend's 2024 point and not its 2025 one, putting a step in the line that comes from the method rather than the data. If any year a series needs lacks counts, the whole series falls back to a plain average — and the page says which happened.

Overlapping bands are flagged, not silently drawn

Size classes are not a partition: SME_10_249 contains SMALL_10_49, and IND_TOTAL contains every age band. Comparing a group against one that contains it is a part against its whole, so the page warns on the chart itself.

Absolute counts only when the years line up

The business register trails the survey by a year. Pairing a 2025 percentage with a 2024 business count produces a figure belonging to neither year, so the section removes itself entirely unless both come from the same year.

Withheld figures are marked, not dropped

Eurostat suppresses a cell when too few surveyed firms fall into it — the raw response carries a u "low reliability" flag and no number. Dropping the row would turn "too small to measure" into "apparently nothing". Rows stay, labelled, with the reason on hover.

Rows are ordered by the EU27 figure, not the selection

Sorting on the selected country would reshuffle rows every time the reader switches country, making two screenshots impossible to compare. The aggregate keeps the running order fixed while the bars move.

Two things the data cannot do

LimitConsequence
The ICT surveys sample only enterprises with 10+ employees Micro-enterprises — most firms in Europe — are never measured. Stated in the methodology and in the footer of every page.
Sector is never crossed with size class The Industry view cannot carry an SME cut. Its unit-of-observation banner says so and points at the Companies view.

The sector view also uses only the eleven single-letter NACE sections. The source additionally carries aggregates (C19-C23), divisions (J61) and bespoke groupings (ICT) that overlap one another — charting the full 50-code list together would double-count.

The visual system

Palette

Four categorical colours, chosen by measurement rather than taste and validated against colour-vision-deficiency simulation in both light and dark modes.

SlotLightDarkRole
1#215699#5b93daPrimary selection
2#c02b2b#e05c62Comparison
3#7c4dc7#8f77e0Third series
4#00897b#0ba98bFourth series

Worst adjacent CVD separation is ΔE 15.9 in light mode and 15.1 in dark, against a target of 8 — roughly double. The slot order is the safety mechanism, not decoration: green cannot occupy slot 2, because measured next to the red it fails outright at ΔE 3.4 (the classic red/green collapse). Teal is viable only with violet between it and the red, which is why the order runs blue, red, violet, teal.

Encoding rules

  • Ordered categories get a single-hue ramp — age bands, education levels. The ramp carries the ordering.
  • Nominal categories get one colour — sectors have no natural order, so the all-sectors chart is a single-colour ranking with the selection lifted. A value-ramp on unordered categories would double-encode bar length as hue.
  • Colour follows the entity, never its rank — filtering never repaints the survivors.
  • Every chart has a table view. Three light-mode slots sit below 3:1 contrast on the surface; a readable table is the required relief.

Motion

Bars grow in over 300 ms with the stagger capped at 110 ms. Deliberately short: the page is built to be screenshotted, and a long entrance means a capture taken a moment early shows half-drawn bars — which it did, at the first attempt.

The narrative arc

Sections declare which part of the argument they belong to and are sorted into a fixed order rather than hand-arranged, so a part can never appear twice in the navigation: 

The scale  →  Where it sits  →  Why they don't  →  What is missing underneath  →  Reading it

The sidebar groups by part instead of numbering a flat list, so the shape of the argument is visible before scrolling. A section whose data does not exist for the current selection removes itself rather than rendering an empty shell.
